When Unnati uploaded her documents during counselling verification, that step didn't happen in isolation. Behind it sits an entire policy and a set of national digital systems built over the last few years, some of which decide what counselling bodies are even allowed to ask for, and others which decide how her data is allowed to move once she's handed it over. 

## NEP 2020: The Policy Everything Else Comes From

Before any of the digital systems described on this page existed, there was a policy decision. In 2020, the Indian government replaced its old education policy with a new one, the National Education Policy, or NEP 2020. Almost everything else on this page exists because of specific things this policy asked for.

Two parts of NEP matter most for understanding Superadmission:

- **Multiple entry and exit points.** Under the old system, if a student left a degree halfway, that time and effort was mostly wasted, there was no formal way to walk away with partial credit. NEP asked for a system where a student could exit after one year with a certificate, after two with a diploma, and after three or four with a full degree, and later resume from wherever they left off.
- **A single national target.** NEP set a target for the Gross Enrolment Ratio, the share of eligible-age students actually enrolled in higher education, at 50 percent by 2035. As of now, that number sits at roughly 28.4 percent, meaning the systems handling admission and enrolment have real growth to absorb.

Neither of these two things works without some way to track a student's credits and identity across institutions over time. That requirement is what the rest of this page is actually built to solve.

## What "Digital Public Infrastructure" Actually Means

Two things most people have already used without thinking of them this way: paying someone through UPI, and proving your identity with an Aadhaar number. Both are examples of something called Digital Public Infrastructure, DPI for short.

What makes something DPI, instead of just another app, comes down to three things:

- **It's shared, not owned by one company.** UPI isn't a product made by one bank. Every bank, and every payment app, builds on top of the same shared system.
- **It's a set of rules, not a single service.** DPI defines how systems are allowed to talk to each other, what format data moves in, what gets verified and how. Anyone who follows those rules can build something on top of it.
- **It's meant to be used by everyone, not sold to a few.** The goal is universal access, a farmer and a bank both use the same UPI rails, not two different systems built for two different budgets.

To make NEP's promise of portable credits and portable identity actually work, India needed the same kind of shared infrastructure, but for education. That's what the next four systems are.

## The Four Building Blocks

### APAAR: One ID for a Student's Entire Academic Life

APAAR stands for Automated Permanent Academic Account Registry. Think of it as an ID number, similar in spirit to Aadhaar, but built specifically to follow a student's academic record, not their general identity.

Right now, Unnati has a JEE application number, a separate NEET number if she'd taken that exam too, a school registration number, and she'll get yet another student ID once she joins a college. None of these numbers know about each other. APAAR is meant to replace this pile of disconnected numbers with one ID that stays with a student from school through every degree they ever pursue, opt-in, and separate from (though linkable to) their Aadhaar number.

### ABC: A Bank Account, But for Credits Instead of Money

ABC stands for Academic Bank of Credits. It works exactly like its name suggests, except what gets deposited isn't money, it's course credits. This is the direct technical answer to NEP's multiple entry and exit promise.

If Unnati studies for a semester at one college and later needs to shift to a different one, or takes a certificate course alongside her degree, ABC is meant to store the credits she earned along the way in one place, tied to her APAAR ID. Credit earned at one institution doesn't just vanish the moment she's no longer enrolled there. She can carry it forward, and in principle even redeem it toward a future degree elsewhere.

### NDEAR: The Rulebook Everything Else Follows

NDEAR stands for National Digital Education Architecture. It isn't a product a student ever directly touches. It's the underlying set of standards that APAAR, ABC, DigiLocker, and other education systems all agree to follow, so they can exchange information with each other without every single pair of systems needing a custom-built connection between them.

A useful way to think about it: NDEAR is the agreed shape of the pipes. APAAR, ABC, and DigiLocker are what flows through those pipes.

### DigiLocker: Where Verified Documents Already Live

DigiLocker is a digital storage system where official documents, board marksheets, certificates, sometimes Aadhaar itself, are placed directly by the organisation that issued them, digitally signed, and available for the student to share whenever they're asked for.

This connects directly back to the document verification step from the previous page. When a counselling body or an institute needs to verify Unnati's marksheet, there are two very different ways that can happen: she scans and uploads a photo of a physical certificate herself, which then has to be manually checked by a human, or the document is pulled directly from DigiLocker, already verified and already signed by the board that issued it, requiring no manual check on either end.

## DPDP: The Law Governing All This Data

None of the above matters if there's no rule for how a student's data is allowed to be collected, stored, shared, or deleted. That rule exists, and it's called the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, DPDP for short, passed in 2023.

DPDP sets out a few plain obligations for anyone handling personal data, including Unnati's rank, category, documents, and contact details as they move through counselling:

- **Consent is required**, and has to be specific, not buried in fine print she never reads.
- **Data can only be used for the purpose it was collected for.** A counselling body collecting her category for seat allocation cannot repurpose it for something unrelated without asking again.
- **She has a right to correction and erasure.** If something about her stored data is wrong, or she wants it removed once it's no longer needed, the law gives her a route to ask for that.

Any system proposing to move a student's data between counselling bodies, DigiLocker, and APAAR, the way Superadmission proposes to, has to be built around these obligations from the start, not bolted on afterward.

## The IndiaAI Mission: Where PraveshAI Fits Nationally

The IndiaAI Mission is the government's national program for building out India's AI capability, compute infrastructure, shared datasets, skilling, and a specific focus area called Safe and Trusted AI, which is about making sure AI systems used in important decisions are explainable and don't behave like an unaccountable black box.

This is directly relevant to PraveshAI, the reasoning and seat-allocation engine inside Superadmission. A seat allocation decision affects a real student's future, which puts it squarely in the category of decisions where an opaque, unexplainable AI system would be a genuine problem. PraveshAI's premise, an algorithm whose logic can be inspected and reasoned about rather than a black box, sits deliberately in line with what the Safe and Trusted AI pillar of the IndiaAI Mission is asking for, even though PraveshAI is not a government-built system.

## Where Superadmission Fits

None of the systems above run counselling or match students to seats. APAAR is an identity number. ABC is a credit ledger. NDEAR is a rulebook. DigiLocker is a document vault. DPDP is a legal obligation. The IndiaAI Mission is a national capability program. Not one of them decides who gets which seat, that job still belongs to JoSAA, MCC, state counselling bodies, and university admission cells, exactly as described earlier in this whitepaper.

Superadmission's proposed position is to sit as a coordination layer above these rails, so that every counselling body doesn't have to independently build its own connection to APAAR, its own connection to DigiLocker, its own DPDP-compliant data handling, and its own connection to ABC.

## What This Could Look Like Technically

This is illustrative, meant to show the shape of the idea, not a claim about an existing production system. A unified student record built this way might look something like this:

```json
{
  "student": {
    "apaarId": "1234-5678-9012-3456",
    "name": "Unnati Sharma",
    "category": "General",
    "consent": {
      "grantedFor": ["seat_allocation", "document_verification"],
      "grantedAt": "2026-06-12T10:04:00Z"
    },
    "digilockerDocRefs": [
      { "type": "class_xii_marksheet", "issuedBy": "CBSE", "verified": true },
      { "type": "category_certificate", "issuedBy": "State Government", "verified": true }
    ],
    "abcCreditLedgerId": "ABC-2026-887214",
    "activeCounsellingRecords": [
      { "body": "JoSAA", "rank": 4821, "status": "choice_filling" }
    ]
  }
}
```

The `consent` field above exists specifically because of DPDP, a record of what the student agreed to, and when, rather than an assumption baked silently into the system. The point of the overall structure is that a counselling body only ever needs to ask for one reference, the APAAR ID, and everything else it needs, verified documents, credit history, category status, is already reachable through the rails already described, under the consent the student actually gave, rather than being collected and verified all over again from scratch.

## What Superadmission Is Not

This is worth stating plainly, because the distinction matters. Superadmission is not proposing to become a new government identity system, and it is not attempting to replace APAAR, DigiLocker, ABC, or NDEAR. It does not decide which student gets which seat, that authority stays entirely with JoSAA, MCC, state bodies, and individual institutions. It is not a government-run AI initiative under the IndiaAI Mission, it is a private system choosing to build in line with that mission's stated direction.

Its proposed role is narrower and more specific: to be the layer that makes it easier for the systems that already exist, and the systems the government is already building, to work together for the student standing at the center of all of it.

That student, still Unnati in this whitepaper, is exactly who the next chapter returns to.
